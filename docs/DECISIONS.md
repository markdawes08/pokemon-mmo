# Decisions

## ADR-001 - Battle implementation

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: selected in the eighth pass; all three bounded P03 batches passed. The production direction is source C commands compiled to WASM with an audited TypeScript scheduler, through one server battle interface. The selected adapter skeleton is implemented; full game mechanics and persistence remain later work.

The experiment demonstrates real source accuracy, PP, criticals, Gen III damage/type arithmetic, poison application, switch/faint cleanup, poison/burn residuals and team outcomes for Tackle, Water Gun, Quick Attack and Poison Powder. The host scheduler follows audited source script order, including replacement before or after residuals and immediate terminal stopping. The manifest distinguishes complete functions, selected source branches, projected layouts and noncompiled trace evidence. No GBA execution loop or CPU/GPU emulator is used; the supplied reference stays read-only.

The chosen boundary is explicit: C owns admitted source commands and numeric behavior; TypeScript owns party projections, choice validation, turn/replacement continuation, ordered diagnostic events and logical checkpoint envelopes. `packages/battle-core/src/wasm-adapter.ts` implements all six operations; `tools/battle-spike/engine.ts` wires the private synthetic profile. A logical checkpoint includes host parties/continuation, transition/event sequences, RNG state/draw count and 148 validated C scalar words. No pointers or raw linear-memory image are serialized. Restore reconstructs a fresh instance without reseeding or replaying gameplay. Candidate transitions commit only after success.

Evidence: 15 literal source-derived turn transcripts/19 transitions retain batch-one damage/RNG checks; 37 recovery boundaries and 26 remaining transitions replay across independent builds, interleaved instances and fresh Node processes. The six-method adapter passes 34 golden boundaries, 22 malformed/incompatible envelopes and seven forged/stale accepted-choice cases. Sixty raw C checkpoint rejection cases preserve live state. The restricted translation-unit audit classifies all 98 globals; it is a future-read audit for this four-move profile, not the full source engine. A nonzero selected-move-slot recovery fixture proves why the old chosen priority must survive replacement before residual ordering.

Two independent builds match at 46,120 bytes, SHA256 `3f47b4f5fed993e75f15e67c567f58c35a6b51476d5c8f6f3233c935b5fb4724`, with no imports and fixed 262,144-byte private memory per instance. Ordinary full-contract turn-cycle p95 was 9.28 ms; dense 20-battle waves (25 transitions each) reached 241.71 ms p95. Envelopes are 2,188-2,526 JSON bytes in this workload. The measured 20-probe external-memory increase is about 5.24 MB and returns to baseline after release/short churn; held adapter states are logical snapshots. These are local Windows/Node compute observations, excluding database/network latency and the 60-minute soak. Dense synchronous contract bursts need reduced restore/validation/project churn and scheduling before shared population gates. These costs do not establish the 25/50 ms world-tick targets.

Why select C reuse: all Section 7 feasibility outcomes have bounded evidence, and the remaining extraction boundary is comprehensible. Rewriting mechanics in TypeScript now would discard verified source arithmetic without fixing the measured shared adapter overhead. The fallback condition was not reached within the three focused batches. Do not retain a second production mechanics engine. No actual engineering-hour total was tracked or inferred.

Admission remains `firered-synthetic-singles-v1`, after omitted battle introduction effects. Two synthetic teams of up to two members carry no real species identity or owned assets. Items, abilities, weather, badges, the full move-end interpreter, other moves/statuses, source creature creation, capture, experience and durable outcomes are unsupported. New effects require source dependency/read-write review, meaningful independent fixtures and profile/checkpoint version changes before admission. The private loader binds compatibility to WASM and host implementation hashes; unknown versions fail. Cross-version migration and other OS/compiler combinations are unverified.

The initial adapter permits zero persistent domain effects, including in PvE, and rejects unsupported effect policies. Its two viewer projections whitelist only permitted information; raw diagnostic events must not become room broadcasts. Checkpoints are trusted server data, not signed client saves. `validateChoice` is pure and `advance` requires the complete accepted set; a later service must persist a partial choice submission before acknowledging it. That service also owns authentication, activity fencing, command receipts and atomic snapshot/effect commits.

Before R1, select an explicit legal development team/profile and implement real creature/encounter generation. Even Route 1 requires Keen Eye/Run Away/Guts, Sand Attack/Tail Whip, legal stats/moves/PP, Potion/run/capture and reachable experience/EV/level/move-learning consequences. The remaining dependency inventory names the source entry points. P04 accounts/durable commands, P05 shared authority and P06 battle presentation/save recovery remain open; this decision enables their implementation without claiming a playable battle slice.

Verification: the full nine-stage gate passed at 2026-09-25T21:53:44.992Z, including 74 Vitest, 49 Python, all battle/recovery/contract checks and 25 development-browser scenarios. Final review caught an extreme replacement-turn counter overflow; its regression failed before the guard and passed afterward. Lint, typecheck and the complete battle stage were rerun successfully at 2026-09-25T21:54:47.014394+00:00, recorded separately in the verification report. Browser behavior was unchanged; fifth-pass built-browser/lifecycle evidence is retained as historical. `battle:spike` now rebuilds, runs all focused verifiers and measures the adapter in a clean child process using the pinned local Zig toolchain.

Evidence: `reports/battle-spike-build.json`, `battle-spike-batch-1.json`, `battle-spike-batch-2.json`, `battle-spike-checkpoint-tests.json`, `battle-spike-recovery.json`, `battle-spike-engine.json`, `battle-spike-state-audit.json`, `battle-spike-measurements.json`, `battle-spike-decision-audit.json`, source/dependency reports and the [experiment README](../tools/battle-spike/README.md).

The permanent account-to-character unique constraint prevents duplicate trainers even after a creation receipt is removed.

Transactions compute/write before publishing memory. Rollback preserves the previous state. An uncertain COMMIT discards the driver connection and rechecks its receipt under the same stable row locks before retrying. A publication failure freezes/reloads committed state before acknowledging it. Test hooks execute actual COMMIT/ROLLBACK and then simulate lost acknowledgement; they are not network-partition or machine-crash evidence. Receipt cleanup is not implemented. Restrictive character/receipt FKs prevent accidental cascade deletion of application data.

Verification covers 27 PostgreSQL world groups, 18 real network groups and four built-client world scenarios within 35 passing browser cases. reports/world-*.json, the current verification report and RUNBOOK record the exact evidence and operational limits.

The first implementation uses fresh authenticated admission on reconnect. The plan's sixty-second transport reconnection grace is not implemented; P05-02 stays open for that policy and user experience. A graceful authorized close saves the last finished tile; a sudden process loss can lose ordinary movement since the last checkpoint. The server runs a 50ms tick and 100ms periodic publication, with immediate command acknowledgements. Each movement packet performs a read-only session/lease/generation check inside the character queue. Two-second heartbeats renew the fifteen-second lease; movement packets do not. Source step durations are 16 frames walking, 8 running and 32 for the admitted ledge jump. Source departure and forced-arrival cooldown precede the atomic transfer; the client plays the door sequence after commit, deliberately adding presentation latency.
